## Core Thesis — the Pattern to Prove

The presentation exists to answer one question: a high volume of tickets is being raised — why, and where are they concentrated?

Test the data for an 80/20-style effort pattern: does a small share of tickets (or specific tags/subtags) consume a disproportionate share of total resolution time, while the bulk resolve quickly and cheaply? Compute the real split from the dashboard's data — don't assume it lands exactly at 80/20; report whatever the actual numbers show, phrased the same way (e.g. "X% of tickets account for Y% of total resolution time").

The payoff of the whole talk: name the specific tags/subtags that make up that high-effort tail, and recommend, for each, whether it's a good candidate for automation, a process change, or neither.

## Story Arc — Section by Section

Build the script around this sequence, each beat tied to a real part of the dashboard:

1. **Opening hook (~30 sec).** One sentence stating the scale of the problem, straight from the Home page tiles — total tickets and unique tickets.
2. **Where it's coming from (~1.5 min).** Tagging page: the top tags/subtags by volume per dataset, and which platform — R1 or R2_NonProd — is driving the most volume.
3. **The effort split — the core finding (~3 min).** Time Bottleneck Analysis: Resolution Summary, Top Bottleneck Detail, and Top Delayed Cases. State the real effort/time split found in the data, and name the specific tag/subtag combinations responsible for it.
4. **Workload context (~1 min).** Workload by Assignee: is that long tail concentrated on specific people, or spread evenly across the team? Ties the effort-split finding to team capacity.
5. **Recommendations (~2 min).** For each named long-tail tag/subtag: automation candidate, process change, or neither — and why, grounded in what the data shows about that specific category.
6. **Close (~30 sec).** One sentence stating the actual ask — what Chirag wants his manager to approve, support, or decide next.

## Deliverable Format

Produce:

1. A full word-for-word script for the full ~10 minutes, in Chirag's own voice — direct, technical, no corporate filler — written so it can be rehearsed from as-is.
2. A trimmed version of the same structure as on-screen talking points (short phrases, not full sentences) per section, for use live in the room instead of reading verbatim.
3. An Anticipated Questions section: 4–6 technical questions the manager or support-function head are likely to ask (how duplicates were handled, what counts as a "measurable" ticket, why some tickets fall outside the measured coverage, how confident the automation recommendation is), each with a one- or two-line confident answer grounded in the dashboard's own data-integrity rules.

Keep the full script to roughly what a person can actually say in 10 minutes — about 1,200–1,500 spoken words. Don't pad it to look thorough.
